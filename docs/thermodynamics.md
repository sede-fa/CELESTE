# Thermodynamic property models

| Model | Class | Phases | Evaluation | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NASA-9 ideal gas / ideal mixture | `thermo.Thermo`, `media.Medium_NASAbase` | gas; condensed species as separate pseudo-species (`H2O(L)`) | NumPy | the default: fast, 1895 species |
| Peng–Robinson + NASA ideal gas | `thermo.Thermo_PR`, `media.Medium_PRbase` | gas, liquid, two-phase (flash) | JAX | high pressure, condensation, separation |
| CoolProp | `thermo.Thermo_CP`, `media.Medium_CPbase` | as specified | CoolProp | reference-quality pure fluids |
| FT correlations | `FT.thermoFT.Thermo`, `FT.mediaFT` | gas / liquid by flag | NumPy | Fischer–Tropsch hydrocarbons up to C50 |

All models share the enthalpy reference of the NASA data (elements at
298.15 K), so heat of reaction is part of `h` and energy balances may combine
different media.

## NASA media

`cp`, `h`, `s` of every species come from the NASA-9 polynomials
(`celeste/data/coeffsData.json`); the mixture is ideal:

    h = Σ x_i h_i(T)          s = Σ x_i s_i(T) − R Σ_gas x_i ln(x_i P / 1 bar)

- `H2O(L)` is a separate condensed phase: no mixing and no pressure term.
- **Outside the fitted range** of a species (e.g. `H2O(L)`: 273.15–600 K) the heat
  capacity is frozen at the range limit; `h` and `s` stay continuous.
  `medium.in_range` tells whether the current temperature is inside the fit of
  all species. Results outside the range are an extrapolation.
- The same kernel (`celeste.jx.nasa`) runs under NumPy inside the media and
  under JAX in `celeste.jx.ideal`.

Lower heating values are tabulated for the common fuels and otherwise computed
from the enthalpies of formation (C/H/O/N species); species without carbon and
hydrogen have an LHV of zero.

## Peng–Robinson media

    P = R T / (v − b) − a(T) / (v² + 2 b v − b²)

- **Alpha function**: Mathias–Copeman below the critical temperature, with
  parameters fitted to the saturation pressure of the reference equations of
  state (maximum error below 1 % for most species, 2 % for ethanol);
  Boston–Mathias above it. Parameters: `celeste/data/eos_species.csv`,
  generated by `tools/build_eos_table.py`.
- **Mixing rules**: van der Waals one-fluid with binary interaction parameters.
  `celeste.jx.eos.DEFAULT_KIJ` holds literature values for common
  hydrocarbon / CO2 / N2 / H2 pairs; pairs with water or methanol are indicative
  only. Pass your own with `kij={('CO2', 'H2O'): 0.12}` or a full matrix.
- **Properties**: `h = h_ideal(T, x) + h_departure`, likewise `s`.
- **Phases**: with `phase='auto'` (default) the state is flashed; `beta`,
  `state`, `x_liquid`, `y_vapour` describe the result. `phase='vapour'` or
  `'liquid'` evaluates that phase without a flash.

Species available: `celeste.jx.eos.species_available()` – H2, N2, O2, Ar, CO,
CO2, H2O, NH3, CH4, C2H6, C2H4, C3H8, C4H10, CH3OH, C2H5OH, CH3OCH3. Add a
species by adding a line to `SPECIES` in `tools/build_eos_table.py` and running
the script.

### Accuracy

Compared with reference equations of state:

| Property | Typical deviation |
|---|---|
| saturation pressure (water, methanol, ammonia, CO2) | < 1 % |
| enthalpy of vaporisation | 1–3 % up to T_r ≈ 0.8, up to 10 % near the critical point |
| gas-phase compressibility and departure enthalpy at high pressure | 1–10 % |
| **liquid density** | −15 to −20 % for water (a known weakness of cubic equations) |

Do not size equipment on Peng–Robinson liquid densities. The NASA media use a
Rackett correlation for liquid water and methanol instead.

The first use of a species set compiles the property functions (2–5 s); later
calls take about 0.1 ms for a property and 0.3 ms for a flash.

## Exergy

`Flange.Ex` is the molar exergy (physical + chemical) relative to
T0 = 298.15 K, P0 = 1 bar and the reference environment behind the standard
chemical exergies in `celeste/data/Chem_exergies.csv`.

- **NASA and FT media** (`FT.thermoFT.ThermoExergy`): physical exergy with the
  dead state reached at T0, P0, where steam above its saturation fraction
  condenses; chemical exergy from the dead-state composition. Dead-state
  properties come from the property model of the medium.
- **Peng–Robinson media** (`Thermo_PR.exergy`):

      ex = h − T0 s − Σ z_i (g°_i − ex°_i)

  with `g°_i` the Gibbs energy of the pure ideal gas at T0, P0 and `ex°_i` its
  standard chemical exergy. Phase changes are contained in `h` and `s`.

Both formulations agree in the ideal-gas limit (tested to 0.02 kJ/mol).

Evaluating `Ex` does not change the stream.
